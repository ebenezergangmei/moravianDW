The tradition of sharing a daily Biblical "Watchword" among the residents of Herrnhut began in 1722. Originally, these texts were passed from house to house by word of mouth to provide a shared focus for daily meditation and conduct. After eight years of this oral tradition, a full year's series of texts, supplemented by hymn verses, was printed for 1731. This marked the beginning of a legacy that continues in this edition. Every year in Herrnhut, Old Testament texts are drawn by lot on behalf of the worldwide Unitas Fratrum, and a complementary New Testament text is added. These daily texts are then distributed to each Moravian Province and adapted for local use. Now translated into over fifty languages, the Text Book is widely used and cherished well beyond the Moravian Church. As Moravians, we view this expansive ministry as a divine privilege and offer praise for this opportunity to glorify His name.

The biblical passages in this edition are primarily drawn from the New Revised Standard Version (used by permission of the Division of Christian Education of the National Council of Churches of the USA), with any alternative translations clearly noted.

In a departure from previous editions, this year's material was compiled by a diverse team of contributors rather than a single editor. The team was encouraged to incorporate prayers, poetry, quotes, and verses to enrich the meaning of the daily texts. The editing panel hopes this fresh, varied approach will engage readers in new ways and wishes to express their gratitude to all contributors for their time and effort. The included hymn verses are sourced predominantly from Moravian traditions, as well as other denominations, and are fully referenced in the year-end index. Where applicable, copyright has been strictly observed, and moon phase information is reproduced under license from HM Nautical Almanac Office (Crown Copyright).

Sunday scripture readings align with the lectionary used by most Anglican churches in Britain and, for the first time, feature the Old Testament narrative reading. The midweek reading schedule is designed to guide readers through the majority of the Bible over a period of roughly two and a half years. To ensure a smooth reading experience, highly repetitive or obscure passages—such as genealogies, detailed legal procedures, construction records, and overlapping sections in Samuel, Kings, and Chronicles—have been largely omitted or addressed through alternative cycles. With the exception of a few complex passages in Revelation, the New Testament is covered in its entirety.

It is our earnest prayer that this book will speak directly to your life and circumstances, guiding you toward a deeper, ever-enriching knowledge of the Bible. May the shared daily texts foster a profound sense of global unity, nourishing our fellowship with the worldwide family of God, no matter where or how we live.
